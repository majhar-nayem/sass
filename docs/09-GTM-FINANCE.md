## 1. Australian launch strategy

### Start in Adelaide, and only Adelaide

Not because Adelaide is the biggest market — because it is the one where you can **stand in the shop**. The first 20 customers must be acquired in person. A small city with dense trade networks and a strong word-of-mouth culture is close to ideal for a wedge, and you already live in it.

Everything about the first 20 sales should be unscalable: you build the site before the pitch, you walk in, you show them their own business on your laptop, you set it up for them, you give them your mobile number. That is not a growth strategy — it is **customer research that happens to generate revenue**, and it is the only way to find out what the product is actually missing before you spend money telling strangers about it.

### The five channels, in the order to use them

**1. Walk in with the site already built (weeks 9–11) — the primary channel.**
Find businesses with a dead or missing website: Google Maps for "plumber Adelaide" and click every result with no site or a broken one; drive down Prospect Road, Torrensville, Port Road, Gouger Street and look at the shopfronts. Build their site in 15 minutes before you go. Walk in mid-morning or mid-afternoon, never at lunch. Open with *"I made you something, can I show you for two minutes?"* and turn the laptop around.

Expect: ~15 doors/day, ~6 conversations, ~2 genuinely interested, ~1 close. That is roughly **20 customers over three weeks of half-days**, and it is the whole minimum target.

**2. Referrals — the compounding one.** Ask every customer, at the moment their site goes live and they are happiest: *"Who else do you know who needs this?"* Offer a month free for both sides. In trades and in migrant business communities a referral is worth more than any ad — one satisfied halal butcher in Mile End is an introduction to a dozen more businesses.

**3. Community and trade networks.** Business SA, local chambers of commerce, the Adelaide Halal Business Network and multicultural chambers, trade Facebook groups ("Adelaide Tradies", "SA Small Business"), Master Plumbers SA / NECA SA newsletters, the Central Market traders. Offer a free talk: *"Getting your business online before Christmas"* — 20 owners in a room beats 2,000 impressions.

**4. Industry landing pages + local SEO (slow burn, start week 8).**
`/ai-website-for-plumbers` · `electricians` · `builders` · `cleaners` · `barbers` · `cafes` · `restaurants` · `butchers` · `tradies` · `adelaide-small-business`
Each with a real generated example, the industry's actual language, and Adelaide-specific proof. These will not produce a customer in 2026. They compound through 2027, and they cost you a weekend.

**5. Paid ads — deliberately last, and not before December.**
Do not spend on ads until you know your close rate, your churn and what customers actually say when they say yes. Spending money to acquire strangers before you can convert warm walk-ins is how a A$5k budget disappears with nothing learned. When you do start: Google Search on `[trade] website adelaide`, A$20/day, one campaign.

### Proof assets to build (week 8, half a day each)
A 60-second screen recording of a real site being generated · 6 real customer sites as a portfolio · one written case study with a number in it ("Dave got 7 enquiries in his first fortnight") · a live public demo anyone can try without signing up.

**The live demo without signup is the highest-converting asset you will build.** Let people type their business name and watch a site appear. Gate the *publish*, never the *magic*.

## 2. Pricing experiment

### What to launch with

| Plan | Price | For |
|---|---|---|
| **Founding Member** | **A$39/mo**, locked 12 months, first 50 only | The entire early market |
| **Business** | A$49/mo | Everyone after the first 50 |
| **Store** | A$99/mo | Products, cart, Stripe Connect, orders |
| **Setup — Express** | A$399 one-off | You build it, 3 revisions, domain connected, live in 48 h |
| **Setup — Store** | A$899 one-off | Above + up to 40 products loaded, Stripe connected, Christmas pack |

### Three deliberate departures from the brief

**1. Kill the A$19–29 Starter tier.** At 45 customers, support runs ~18 h/month — around 0.4 h per customer. A A$19 customer consumes the same support as a A$99 customer while contributing less than the founder's hourly cost of serving them. A cheap tier does not expand the market here; it fills it with the customers most likely to churn and most likely to need help. Floor at A$39.

**2. Fold Pro into Business.** Four plans is a decision the customer has to make, and a decision is friction. A tradie choosing between Business and Pro chooses neither, that day. Two plans plus a Store plan is the right number when your differentiator is *simplicity*.

**3. Lead with the setup fee, not the subscription.** See §3 — it is 82% of first-quarter revenue.

### How to actually run the experiment

At n=50 you **cannot** get statistical significance on price. Do not A/B test; you would need thousands of visitors to detect anything, and you will conclude nonsense from noise. Run it sequentially and qualitatively instead:

- Customers 1–20: A$39 founding. Record every objection **verbatim**.
- Customers 21–50: A$49. Compare close rate and the *texture* of the objections.
- Throughout, offer the setup fee to every retail and hospitality prospect and record the take-up rate.

**The signal to watch is not the close rate — it is how they say yes.** An instant yes at A$39 means you are underpriced. A long pause followed by "…yeah alright" is roughly the right price. "How much?!" means wrong customer, not wrong price.

Also test **annual prepay at 2 months free (A$390/yr)** from day one. Every annual customer is a January churn risk removed, and December cash you can use.

## 3. Three-month financial model (AUD)

| Period | Cust | Exit MRR | Subs cash | Setup fees | Total in | Costs | Net | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 · Sep 8 – Oct 7 | 0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $1,510 | −$1,510 | −$1,510 |
| M2 · Oct 8 – Nov 7 | 10 | $450 | $225 | $1,650 | $1,875 | $608 | $1,267 | −$243 |
| M3 · Nov 8 – Dec 8 | 45 | $2,175 | $1,371 | $5,580 | $6,951 | $1,129 | $5,822 | **$5,580** |
| M4 · Dec 9 – Jan 8 ⚠ | 42 | $1,998 | $2,283 | $500 | $2,783 | $862 | $1,921 | $7,500 |

Assumptions: 85% on A$39 founding / 15% on A$99 Store → **ARPU A$48**. Stripe 1.75% + $0.30. M1 costs include ~A$1,200 one-off legal. 12 done-for-you setups across M2–M3 at ~A$600 average. Churn: 0 in M2, 3 in M3, **7 in M4**.

### The finding that should change your behaviour

**Setup fees are A$7,230 of A$8,826 total revenue — 82% of everything you earn in the first three months.**

Subscriptions are almost irrelevant to 2026 cash. They matter enormously to 2027 and to whether this is a business at all, but for the next 13 weeks the thing that pays for the infrastructure, the legal advice and your time is **done-for-you setup at A$399–899**.

Three consequences:

1. **Sell the setup fee first, every time.** "A$899 and it's done, then A$99/month to keep it running" outperforms "A$99/month" for a retailer in October — it reads as buying a thing rather than signing up to something.
2. Done-for-you is also your best research. You will see exactly where the product fails, on a real business, in real time, twelve times.
3. **It does not scale, and that's fine.** 12 setups is ~30 hours. 100 would be a job, not a company. Use it deliberately to fund and inform the first quarter, and design the product so it becomes unnecessary.

### Unit economics at 45 customers

| | Per customer/mo |
|---|---|
| ARPU | **A$48.33** |
| AI (Anthropic) | A$0.75 |
| Infrastructure (fixed ÷ 45) | A$12.36 |
| Stripe | A$1.15 |
| Email/storage | A$0.35 |
| **Gross margin** | **A$33.72 (70%)** |

Margin is 70% only because fixed infrastructure is spread across 45 customers. It scales sharply: **~79% at 100 customers, ~87% at 250.** The A$100–500/month infrastructure target in the brief holds through Month 2 and is exceeded by ~A$56 in Month 3 — covered 6× by revenue, and reducible below A$400 with the AI levers in `04-AI-LAYER.md` §6.

**The real cost is not in that table.** Support at 0.4 h/customer/month is 18 h/month at 45 customers — roughly A$1,080 of founder time against A$2,175 of MRR. At 150 customers it is 60 h/month, which is a full-time job that nobody is paying for. **Support load, not AI cost, is what caps this business without either self-serve improvements or a hire.** Instrument it from customer one: log every support contact and its cause, and fix the top cause every fortnight.

### The January cliff — plan for it in November

Australia closes from ~20 December to ~20 January. New sales go to roughly zero and Christmas-motivated buyers churn once their season ends. The model assumes **7 churned in M4 against 4 new** — a first net-negative month, and it is not a failure signal, it is the calendar.

Mitigations, all of which must be in place **before** mid-December:
- Annual prepay pushed hard in November (2 months free).
- The 27 December Christmas-teardown email (`08-CHRISTMAS.md` §2) — an out-of-season site is a cancellation trigger.
- The weekly digest email running through January so the value stays visible while the owner is at the beach.
- A January campaign aimed at a different motivation entirely: *"New financial year, new website"* and the February trade rush.
- Treat any customer who bought purely for Christmas as a 3-month customer in your own forecasting, and be pleasantly surprised.

## 4. Risks and mitigations

| # | Risk | L | I | Mitigation | Early warning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Scope exceeds solo capacity** (~35% over before selling) | **High** | **High** | Ship-10 core is unconditional (weeks 1–7); the fork is a week-8 hiring decision, made by 25 Sep. See `01-ROADMAP.md` §0. | Week 4 gate missed |
| R2 | **Nobody buys** — website apathy, not price | Medium | **Fatal** | **Week 5: a 6-hour demand probe** — 10 doors with generated sites, before the other 180 hours are spent. Then 6 full pilots at week 8. | <2 leaning forward at the week-5 probe |
| R3 | **Christmas window missed** | Medium | High | Christmas ships week 8; selling window is 1–15 Nov. | Week 7 slips |
| R4 | **January churn cliff** | **High** | Medium | Annual prepay, teardown email, digest, Jan campaign. Modelled, not hoped. | Cancel reasons in December |
| R5 | **Generation quality is merely OK** | Medium | **High** | Weekly manual review of 10 sites. Templates as the floor. If AI output is worse than a good template, the AI is a feature, not the product. | Pilots asking for lots of edits |
| R6 | **Renderer outage takes every site down at once** | Low | **Fatal** | 2+ machines, Cloudflare cache serves stale, phone alert on 5xx, rollback runbook. | Any renderer 500 |
| R7 | **Runaway AI spend** | Medium | Medium | Quota + per-org cap + platform circuit breaker in week 4. | Daily spend alert |
| R8 | **Cross-tenant data leak** | Low | **Fatal** | 4 isolation layers + generated isolation test in week 2. | — |
| R9 | **ACL breach from AI-generated claims** | Medium | High | Hard rules + banned-claims validator + `source:customer_supplied`. Validated: see `tests/spec-guardrails.mjs`. | Eval harness |
| R10 | **Wix/Squarespace/GoDaddy AI builders** | **Certain** | Medium | They already exist and are fine. Do not compete on AI. Compete on **an Adelaide person who answers the phone, Australian defaults, and done-for-you setup** — things a global platform structurally cannot offer. | — |
| R11 | **Support load caps growth** | **High** | Medium | Log every contact + cause; fix the top cause fortnightly. Self-serve domain wizard is the biggest single win. | Hours/customer rising |
| R12 | **Google treats generated sites as thin content** | Medium | Medium | Real business facts, JSON-LD LocalBusiness, unique copy per site, proper sitemaps. Never generate near-duplicate copy across tenants — vary structure, not just names. | Indexation checks |
| R13 | **Tenant publishes illegal/harmful content on your domain** | Medium | Medium | AUP, abuse@, publish-time scan, takedown runbook. | — |
| R14 | **Founder attention split** (parallel projects) | **High** | High | 13 weeks is not a side project. Decide honestly in week 1 whether it has your Tuesdays and Wednesdays or your Sunday nights. | Week 2 gate slipping |
| R15 | **Stripe Connect onboarding friction** kills store conversions | Medium | Medium | Do it *with* them during setup — it's a 10-minute call and part of the A$899. | Store signups stalling at Connect |

### The three that actually decide the outcome

**R1 (capacity)** and **R2 (demand)** are the only ones that can kill this in 13 weeks, and both are answerable early and cheaply. Capacity shows itself at the week-2 gate. Demand shows itself in week 5, for six hours of your time, by walking ten generated sites into ten real businesses — long before the remaining 180 hours are committed. Structuring the plan so the two fatal risks are tested first, for almost nothing, matters more than any individual feature in it.

**R14** is the one nobody writes down. This plan needs ~35 focused hours a week for 13 consecutive weeks, in a quarter that also contains everything else. If that is not real, choose Ship-10 now and succeed at something smaller rather than fail at all of it.
