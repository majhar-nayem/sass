# Ship-10 — The Smallest Version That Gets 10 Australian Businesses Paying

> *"What is the smallest version of this product that can realistically get 10 Australian businesses to pay within 3 months?"*

This is the answer to the brief's closing question, and it is the plan to actually build.

---

## The one-sentence answer

**A single-page website generator for Adelaide tradies and cafés: 10 components, 2 templates, chat editing, a contact form that emails them, and a subdomain — with custom domains and Christmas commerce done by hand for the customers who need them.**

Roughly **seven weeks of build**, not thirteen. Everything else in the brief is the *second* product, and it is far easier to build once ten people are paying and telling you what's wrong.

## Why smaller is the higher-probability path

The full brief needs ~610 developer hours against ~455 available (`01-ROADMAP.md` §0). Attempting it means arriving at 1 December with everything 80% done, which is worth nothing. Ship-10 needs ~290 hours, leaving **~165 hours for selling, support and the things you cannot predict** — and the things you cannot predict are what determine whether ten people pay.

The brief's own §18 says it: *"Do not spend months building features before reaching the first 10 paying customers."* Ship-10 is that sentence taken literally.

### The one thing not to cut

**Publish must feel instant, and the site must look genuinely good on a phone.** The entire proposition is "20 minutes and it's live". If generation takes 90 seconds and the result looks like a 2011 template, no amount of feature coverage saves it. Spend the saved hours on the 10 components being *excellent* rather than 31 being adequate.

## Getting to 10

Ten customers is **ten conversations that go well**, not a funnel. At a ~1-in-7 close rate on warm in-person pitches, that is ~70 doors — about **two weeks of half-days**.

The mechanic:
1. Google Maps → "plumber Adelaide", "café Prospect", "barber Norwood". Note everyone with no website or a dead one.
2. **Build their site before you visit.** 15 minutes. It is the entire pitch.
3. Walk in mid-morning. *"I made you something — can I show you for two minutes?"* Turn the laptop around.
4. *"It's live today if you want it. A$39 a month. I'll set your domain up myself."*
5. Get their photos and real details on the spot, publish while you're standing there.
6. Ask who else needs one. Every time.

Ten customers at A$39 is A$390 MRR. **That is not the point.** The point is ten businesses who will tell you what to build next, six of whom will refer someone, and proof that an Australian small business will hand over a card for this.

## What "done" looks like on 8 December

- 10+ paying customers, live sites, at least 3 on custom domains
- Every one of them has received a real enquiry through their site
- A written list of the top 10 things they asked for that you couldn't do — **this is your Q1 2027 roadmap, and it is worth more than the MRR**
- Renderer uptime ≥ 99.9% through December
- You know your close rate, your support hours per customer, and what they say when they say yes

Then build the rest of the brief — ecommerce, custom domain self-serve, multi-page, the full component library — for customers who already exist and are already paying, in a January and February when nothing is on fire and nobody's Christmas depends on it.

## The honest summary

The brief describes a genuinely good product and the architecture is right — spec-driven generation with a controlled renderer is the correct call, and the multi-tenant model is sound. The risk is not the design. **The risk is that thirteen weeks of solo building against a deadline that cannot move produces something 80% finished in every direction.**

Build the narrow thing. Sell it in person. Let ten paying customers tell you which of the other 27 deliverables actually matter.
